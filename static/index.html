Evidence-first company research

AI sounds sure, even when it’s wrong.

Receipts checks what an AI wrote against the live web and keeps only the claims that can show a receipt: a real source, and the exact words that prove it.

See how it works

The AI proposes. Plain code decides. Anything it can’t prove is flagged, never guessed.

Confident AI text is already getting caught out in public.

When people paste AI output into reports and emails without checking it, wrong facts and fake references travel with it. Recent cases, as reported:

Deloitte Australia

A government report worth A$440,000 was reported to contain references to research that doesn’t exist, and a made-up quote from a court judgment. Deloitte agreed to a partial refund.

AP News, October 2025

EY

EY withdrew a study on loyalty programmes after researchers found apparent AI hallucinations in it.

Australian Financial Review, May 2026

KPMG

KPMG pulled an AI report after its sources were challenged. The detection firm GPTZero said only 5 of the report’s 45 citations matched their sources.

The Register, June 2026

These are press reports, not our findings, and Receipts accuses no one. They show the same failure: confident text with nothing behind it. Receipts is a check you can run before you hit send.

Follow one claim through

Say an AI-written briefing contains this sentence.

  1. 1

    Read

    Receipts pulls out every checkable fact: numbers, dates, names.

    Amazon has invested $8 billion in Anthropic in total.
  2. 2

    Check

    It searches the live web. The AI picks the best source and copies the exact words it relies on. That is only a proposal.

    Search the web→AI proposes a source and a quote

  3. 3

    Prove

    Plain code, not AI, makes the call. For a claim a source disagrees with, it checks:

    • The source really came back from the search
    • The quote really appears in that source
    • The source is about the same company
    • The quote isn’t a rumour or a plan

    ✕ Contradicted: a source says about $18 billion.

    For a claim to count as supported, code also checks that every number and date in the claim appears in the quote.

Every claim ends in one of four states

✓ Supported
A real source states it, and code verified the quote.
✕ Contradicted
A source says something different. The claim is struck out.
◷ Plan, not a fact
It is about the future, and the source says the same. We don’t call it fact.
? Needs your check
No source we could verify. Never guessed; handed back to you.

    What it can’t do (yet)

    We would rather tell you than have you find out.

    • It checks that a source says it, not that the source is trustworthy. Some receipts come from weak websites.
    • It plays safe. It sometimes sends a true claim back to you as “needs your check”.
    • It only knows what the web search returns. No result means no receipt, not “false”.
    • The rewrite check covers numbers and dates, not every word of the prose.
    • It doesn’t yet check a report’s reference list. That is next.
